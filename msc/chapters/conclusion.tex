\chapter{Concluding Remarks}
\label{conclusion}

This thesis investigated reporting deadlocks as runtime exceptions to simplify diagnosis.
After reviewing static, dynamic, and hybrid approaches, we manually classified deadlock reports from open source projects.
The prevalence of deadlocks involving two threads and two locks (\ac{tttl}) motivated a lightweight extension of Java's \emph{ReentrantLock} that detects these deadlocks and raises exceptions in both threads.
Student experiments showed faster bug identification in unfamiliar code and suggested greater benefits for less experienced developers.

\section{Research Contributions}

The main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Confirmed prior findings that \ac{tttl} deadlocks predominate: they accounted for 92.07\% of identified resource deadlocks.
\item Extended {\tt ReentrantLock} to detect \ac{tttl} deadlocks and raise runtime exceptions in both threads.
\item Measured overhead with a conservative benchmark and estimated an execution-time impact below 6\% in the worst case for the 12 profiled systems discussed in the evaluation, excluding systems dominated by critical sections.
\item Presented a correctness proof sketch for the detection protocol.
\item Demonstrated reduced bug-identification time in student experiments.
\item Found evidence suggesting greater benefits for less experienced developers, a hypothesis requiring further study.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}

Future work includes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Extending detection to synchronized blocks, which appeared frequently in the bug reports.
\item Adding thread and lock context to {\tt DeadlockException} messages while keeping overhead low.
\item Analyzing the collected but unused comment-count and resolution-time data, with comparable non-deadlock reports, to assess the relative difficulty of deadlock bugs.
\item Repeating student experiments with groups stratified by concurrent-programming experience to test whether less experienced developers benefit more.
\item Studying projects in other popular languages to compare the prevalence of \ac{tttl} deadlocks.
\end{enumerate}
